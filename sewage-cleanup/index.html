A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Logan, Logan Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor

Sewage Cleanup FAQ

Sewage backups are extremely dangerous. Raw sewage contains bacteria, viruses and parasites that can cause serious illness, including hepatitis, E. coli infection and respiratory disease. The emergency phase, meaning sewage removal plus decontamination, generally lasts 1 to 3 days. If materials have to be replaced, full restoration can stretch to 1 to 3 weeks, based on how much was damaged. Throughout, the focus is on returning your home to a safe, livable state without delay.
What each item is made of decides that. Glass, sealed tile, metal and other hard surfaces that do not absorb water are normally salvageable after disinfection. Upholstery, mattresses, carpet with its pad, insulation and drywall soak up sewage and cannot be made safe, so they are thrown out. We document what is removed so you have a record for your insurance claim.
Standard homeowner's insurance often does NOT cover sewage backup unless you have a specific sewage or water backup endorsement. Adding that endorsement is something we strongly recommend: it typically costs little, about $50 to $100 a year, and the protection is critical. Look at your policy documents, or call your agent to confirm.
Keep people and pets out of the affected area. Stop using sinks, toilets and appliances that drain, since more water makes the backup worse. If the panel and thermostat can be reached without contact with sewage, shut off electricity to that area and stop the air system. Leave the cleanup alone, and wash well with soap if any got on your skin.
